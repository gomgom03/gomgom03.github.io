About

I'm a Ph.D. student in Computer Science at Rice University, advised by Prof. Lydia Kavraki, and an NSF Graduate Research Fellow.

I work where computational structural biology meets generative AI. Structure predictors give a single static snapshot of a protein, but function depends on how it moves. I develop physics-informed, diffusion-based models that turn static structures into biologically relevant conformational ensembles and transition pathways. Applications include judging how useful predicted structures are, understanding protein–protein interactions such as pMHC–TCR recognition, and rational protein and antibody design.

Before my Ph.D., I earned B.S. degrees in Computer Science and Biosciences at Rice, where I built bioinformatics tools in Todd Treangen's lab and worked on single-cell imaging analysis at CellChorus.
